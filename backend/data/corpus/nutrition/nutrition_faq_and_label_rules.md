# Nutrition FAQ and Label Rules (demo)

## 1. What the numbers mean
Calories are reported in kcal, protein in grams, sodium in milligrams. The matrix gives one standard portion per ingredient on a footlong. Values are rounded to the nearest 5 kcal and nearest 10 mg of sodium in the dataset, but totals are exact sums of the listed rounded values.

## 2. Portion rules
- Bread: one footlong loaf.
- Protein: one standard portion unless the order says double.
- Cheese: one standard portion (two slices) unless the order says double.
- Sauces: one standard serving per sauce named. Two sauces on an order are counted separately.
- Toppings: one standard portion each.
Customers cannot reduce a portion below the standard portion in this demo.

## 3. Sodium guidance
The strict diet cap uses a sodium limit well below an adult's typical daily intake. A footlong with a processed meat, a cheese and two sauces can exceed the limit in a single sandwich. Items with a high sodium share are Turkey Breast, Italian Herbs & Cheese bread and pickles.

## 4. Protein guidance
The protein floor is strict: a total of exactly 55 g does not satisfy "more than 55 g". Chicken and steak proteins carry the most protein per portion. Cheese contributes a few grams per portion.

## 5. Allergen labels
The allergen column lists contained allergens. The strict dairy rule uses the milk allergen only. Egg is not dairy. A label such as "Cheese" in the name of a bread signals that the bread itself contains milk. A sauce can also contain milk even when its name does not say so, so check the matrix.

## 6. Frequently asked questions
- Can I swap a dairy item to meet a zero-dairy cap? Yes: choose a bread, cheese and sauce without milk. The audit reports the order as it was placed.
- Are the numbers official? No. They are illustrative and self-contained for this demo.
- Do toppings count toward sodium? Yes. Pickles carry noticeable sodium.
- Does toasting change the totals? No, toasting is not modelled.
