# Order Audit Worked Examples (footlong, illustrative)

## 1. How to read these examples
Each example uses the footlong ingredient matrix and the strict diet cap policy (calories strictly below 950 kcal, protein strictly above 55 g, sodium strictly below 1,800 mg, zero dairy). They show the method, not the order you are asked to audit. Totals are sums of the matrix rows.

## 2. Example A: lean turkey footlong
Order: Multigrain bread, Turkey Breast, no cheese, Yellow Mustard, lettuce, tomatoes, spinach.
- Calories: 420 + 180 + 0 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 5 = 635 kcal. Below 950: PASS.
- Protein: 17 + 32 + 0 + 0 + 1 + 0 + 1 = 51 g. Not above 55: FAIL by 4 g.
- Sodium: 620 + 1060 + 0 + 180 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 1,890 mg. Not below 1,800: FAIL by 90 mg.
- Dairy: no ingredient lists milk: PASS.
Result: two violations (protein and sodium); calories and dairy pass.

## 3. Example B: chicken and oil footlong
Order: Italian (white) bread, Rotisserie-Style Chicken, Oil & Vinegar, lettuce, tomatoes, spinach, jalapenos.
- Calories: 400 + 200 + 45 + 10 + 10 + 5 + 5 = 675 kcal. PASS.
- Protein: 14 + 38 + 0 + 1 + 0 + 1 + 0 = 54 g. Not strictly above 55: FAIL by 1 g.
- Sodium: 640 + 520 + 5 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 120 = 1,315 mg. PASS.
- Dairy: bread has wheat and soy only; nothing lists milk: PASS.
Result: one violation (protein). Note that soy appears in the bread, which is an allergen but not a dairy violation.

## 4. Example C: a doubled cheese order
Doubling a cheese doubles its three numbers. Two portions of American cheese add 180 kcal, 10 g protein and 760 mg sodium, and the dairy rule fails because American cheese lists milk. A doubled cheese is the quickest way to break a sodium cap.

## 5. Common mistakes
- Using six-inch values for a footlong order.
- Forgetting that a sauce can contain milk and soy.
- Reporting only the largest violation instead of every violation.
- Treating a total exactly equal to the cap as passing.
