# CRA — TFSA Contribution Room, Withdrawals and Common Mistakes

## 1. Contribution room
Each year a person aged 18 or older who is resident in Canada earns new TFSA contribution room. The annual limit was $7,000 for 2025 and 2026. Unused room carries forward indefinitely. Room is reduced by contributions and restored by withdrawals only from the following January 1.

## 2. How an over-contribution happens
People over-contribute by depositing more than their room, by withdrawing and re-depositing within the same year, or by contributing to several TFSA accounts at several institutions. The CRA tracks the total across all accounts.

## 3. Cost of an excess amount
The tax is 1% per month on the highest excess amount in the month. It is charged for each month the excess remains, so a short mistake is cheap and a long one is not. For a $4,500 excess held 3 months, the total is $4,500 x 1% x 3 = $135.00.

## 4. Fixing the problem
Withdraw the excess as soon as possible. The tax then stops accruing for later months. File the TFSA return (RC243) to report the tax for the year. The CRA may waive some or all of the tax when the excess happened by reasonable error and was corrected promptly.

## 5. Withdrawals are not penalised
Taking money out of a TFSA is not taxable and does not reduce future room permanently. Withdrawn amounts are added back to room in January of the next year.

## 6. Checks before contributing
Confirm room in My Account on the CRA website. Use a direct transfer between institutions, not a withdrawal and a deposit. Keep records of contributions made during the year.

## 7. Related penalties
A TFSA also has separate taxes for non-qualified investments and for carrying on a business inside the account. These do not apply to a simple cash over-contribution.
