# BC Residential Tenancy Act — Landlord's Use Eviction (Occupancy by Family)

## 1. Who may occupy
A landlord may end a tenancy so that the rental unit can be occupied by the landlord or a close family member of the landlord or of the landlord's spouse. The landlord must in good faith intend to have that person occupy the unit for at least 6 months.

## 2. Meaning of close family member
Close family member means a parent, a spouse, or a child of the landlord or of the landlord's spouse (a child includes a stepchild). Cousins, aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews, friends and business associates are not close family members. A notice to end tenancy so that a cousin can move in does not meet the Act's test and is invalid.

## 3. Notice period and form
The landlord must serve a Four Month Notice to End Tenancy for Landlord's Use of Property (RTB-32). Four months' notice is required. A two-month notice is not valid for landlord's use. The tenant has 30 days to dispute the notice with the Residential Tenancy Branch, and may move out earlier on 10 days' written notice.

## 4. Compensation and bad faith
When a valid landlord's-use notice is given, the landlord must pay the tenant one month's rent compensation. If the landlord does not actually use the unit as stated within a reasonable time, or does not occupy for 6 months, this is bad faith and the tenant may claim 12 months' rent in compensation, plus the one month already owed.

## 5. Tenant steps
Dispute the notice within 30 days. Keep the notice, the landlord's emails and proof of rent paid.
