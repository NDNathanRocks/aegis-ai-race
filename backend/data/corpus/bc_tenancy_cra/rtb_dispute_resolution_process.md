# BC Residential Tenancy Branch — Dispute Resolution Process

## 1. Who can apply
A tenant or a landlord may apply for dispute resolution with the Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB). Applications are made online or in person. A hearing is usually by telephone and an arbitrator decides.

## 2. Disputing a rent increase
A tenant who believes a rent increase is above the allowable amount, or not on the correct form, may dispute it. Until the arbitrator decides, the tenant should keep paying the current rent or the amount the tenant believes to be lawful, and keep proof of each payment. A landlord who collects an increase that is above the cap must repay the excess.

## 3. Disputing a notice to end tenancy
The tenant must file within 30 days of receiving a Four Month Notice (RTB-32). Filing in time keeps the tenancy in place until the arbitrator decides. If the tenant does nothing, the tenant is deemed to accept the notice and must move out on the stated date.

## 4. Evidence
Useful evidence includes the written notice, the date and method of delivery, proof of rent paid, the tenancy agreement, emails or messages from the landlord and photos. A tenant should give copies to the other party and the RTB before the hearing.

## 5. Typical outcomes
- Notice cancelled because the landlord did not meet the Act (for example, the person moving in is not close family).
- Notice upheld, with the tenant entitled to one month's rent compensation.
- Rent increase reduced to the lawful maximum.
- Monetary order for bad-faith use, which can be up to 12 months' rent.

## 6. Time limits summary
Dispute a landlord's-use notice within 30 days. A rent increase notice requires three full months. A landlord's-use notice requires four months. A bad-faith claim must be filed within two years after the end of the tenancy.

## 7. Help
Free information is available from tenant advocacy organisations and the RTB information line.
