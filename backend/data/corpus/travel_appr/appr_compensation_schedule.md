# Air Passenger Protection Regulations (APPR) — Delay Compensation Schedule

## 1. Scope and carrier size
The Canadian Air Passenger Protection Regulations apply to flights to, from and within Canada. A flight from Vancouver to Toronto is a domestic Canadian flight, so the APPR governs it. A large carrier carried two million passengers or more worldwide in each of the two preceding years. Air Canada is a large carrier; other airlines are small carriers.

## 2. Compensation for delay within the carrier's control (large carrier)
Compensation is owed per passenger, based on the arrival delay at the final destination, when the disruption was within the carrier's control and not required for safety:
- Arrival delay of 3 hours or more but less than 6 hours: CAD $400.
- Arrival delay of 6 hours or more but less than 9 hours: CAD $700.
- Arrival delay of 9 hours or more: CAD $1,000.
On a large carrier, a flight must be delayed 3 hours or more to qualify for APPR compensation. Small carriers pay CAD $125, $250 and $500 for the same three bands. A crew scheduling shortage is within the carrier's control. A crew scheduling shortage is not a safety exemption: the carrier owes the full compensation.

## 3. Safety-required events
Genuine safety events, such as an urgent safety repair, are treated differently from staffing problems. A carrier cannot relabel a scheduling problem as a safety matter to avoid paying.

## 4. How the carrier must pay
Compensation is payable within 30 days of the claim. The passenger is entitled to a cash-equivalent payment (cheque, bank transfer or card refund). The carrier may offer a voucher or points only as an alternative, and only if it states the value, the expiry and that the passenger may choose cash instead. A voucher is never a mandatory substitute for the cash amount.

## 5. Claims
A passenger claims by writing to the airline. If the airline refuses, the passenger may complain to the Canadian Transportation Agency. A passenger travelling in a group is compensated individually: the amount above is multiplied by the number of passengers.
